Learning Alignment from Limited Clinical sEEG Recordings
via Segment Augmentation

Decoding text from neural activity offers a promising communication pathway for speech impaired individuals. However, sentence decoding from clinical stereo-electroencephalography (sEEG) is severely constrained by non-optimal placement and data scarcity, often limited to minutes of recording (5 minutes in this study). At this scale, connectionist temporal classification (CTC)-based decoders perform near chance level, and neural-to-text alignments fall below those of untrained models. To enforce meaningful alignment under severe data constraints, we introduce segment augmentation, which explicitly maps short neural segments to corresponding sub-label sequences. Without modifying CTC objective, decoder architecture, or requiring extra recordings, segment augmentation decreases the phoneme error rate from 0.665 to 0.134 relative to a 0.734 chance baseline. This gain stems from enhanced alignment rather than sample volume; augmenting one-quarter of the corpus outperforms full-corpus training. Across eight clinical recordings, segment augmentation consistently improves alignment intersection over union, achieving up to a 6.12× increase.
